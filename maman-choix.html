Coded, or the cartoon we own

Your rule G19 says two things and here they disagree. "A downloaded cartoon beats a coded shape for anything that does not move" lets these four stay, because all four DO move and replay when she taps them. "A coded triangle is not a mountain to a six-year-old" says the opposite, because a moving triangle is still a triangle. Same size, same cream ground, coded on the left. The coded ones are photographed after their animation has finished, so you are seeing them at their best.

Swapping any of them costs nothing — the cartoons are owned and signed. What it costs is the movement, which cannot be got back from a drawing. Tell me which way, one by one or all four.

This one is coded for a reason worth knowing. Three owned night pictures from an older book match these words almost line for line — a child sitting up in bed waving to her mother, a child asleep under a crescent moon. All three were rendered and opened, and all three draw a DARK-SKINNED CHILD WITH BLACK HAIR. Placing one would put a different child in the chapter. The coded window shows no people at all, which is honest rather than wrong.

And the mountain has nothing to compare

The picture your rule is actually named after.
